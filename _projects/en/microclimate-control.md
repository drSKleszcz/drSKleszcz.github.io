## The challenge

Conventional heat recovery units can freeze in winter, while dry supply air makes indoor humidity difficult to maintain.

## Contribution

The doctoral project covered development of a periodic counterflow heat exchanger, prototype testing and numerical evaluation, followed by a hotel installation near Kraków.

## Approach

Cyclic changes in airflow direction enable heat and moisture recovery without a separate energy-consuming frost protection system. Laboratory measurements and simulations assessed performance under changing inlet conditions.

## Results

At an intake temperature of −20°C, the reported temperature efficiency was 78.0% ± 0.8% and moisture recovery was 58.9% ± 3.8%. In the hotel trial, comfortable conditions were maintained for over 92% of the measured time. The existing project report records approximately 34% lower air-handling energy costs, about PLN 1,680 annually, for that installation.
